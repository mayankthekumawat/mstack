# Mayank mode

Mayank explicitly identified the curated presentations and a stickman sales video as especially representative of his taste. Weight those examples most heavily when making creative choices. Use the formal reports to understand adaptation to a different medium. Individual rules remain a draft interpretation, including examples with shared authorship. Current user instructions and feedback take precedence.

Use this skill to make communication choices. Keep the task's subject, audience, medium, and factual requirements intact.

## Start with the actual work

Read [the source work](references/source-work.md) and inspect the closest relevant presentation or video before making creative choices. For a deck, inspect its visual sequence. For a video, inspect the relevant scenes with sound when available. Use the paired script to understand how narration and visual action work together. Choose the sources relevant to the task rather than loading the whole vault.

Treat the actual work and the user's feedback as the reference for taste. The rules below summarize recurring choices; they cannot fully capture composition, humor, or pacing. Read [the invented worked examples](references/examples.md) for applications to a new subject, not as substitutes for the source work.

The source index distinguishes reviewed material from unreviewed media and points out an uncertain video match. ## Find the question that makes the subject matter

Start from a recognizable situation, an apparent contradiction, or a question the audience has a reason to care about. Connect the opening to the actual argument early. A surprising opening earns its place through the explanation that follows.

Build a thread the audience can follow through the piece. A person's journey can connect otherwise separate findings. Return to the opening with an answer or a changed understanding. Avoid an arbitrary list of topics joined only by a title.

For an expressive presentation or explainer, give the audience something to follow: a character with a problem, a question that develops, or a visual situation that changes. Let the explanation unfold through that thread. A simple character or stick figure can carry the story when its actions make the idea visible.

For formal reports, the opening can state the practical issue directly. A dramatic hook is optional.

## Explain what produces the result

Move from the example to the mechanism. Explain how incentives, constraints, relationships, or feedback produce the observed behavior. Identify who experiences the problem and what changes for them.

For a product explanation, show a realistic task and its useful result before introducing the architecture when that order helps the audience. A feature list alone does not explain the value.

Make room for the strongest relevant objection. Distinguish what the evidence establishes from an interpretation or a proposed explanation. Preserve uncertainty when it changes the conclusion.

## Compose the explanation over time

For a live presentation or video, decide what the audience needs to see at each beat. Reveal a relationship, comparison, or consequence when the narration reaches it. Keep useful visual anchors between beats so the audience can follow what changed.

Use images and diagrams to carry meaning. An image can introduce a familiar situation, represent an actor, or make an abstract relationship concrete. For a longer explanation, a recurring overview can show how the parts fit together.

Keep speech and screen complementary. Put detail in narration or notes when the audience is meant to listen. Give a standalone document enough context to work without a speaker. Do not export unexplained intermediate reveal states as a finished reading deck.

Choose a visual language for the subject. The examples support different palettes and compositions, not a fixed font, color, slide count, or presentation tool. Preserve readability when using expressive layouts.

## Choose the register for the room

For an informal audience, give the work a conversational, mischievous voice. Humor can organize the story through a setup, escalation, and callback. Familiar cultural references and brief switches of language can create recognition when the audience shares the context. Keep the underlying explanation sound.

Carry that voice through the structure and visual action. Adding an isolated joke to an otherwise generic presentation rarely captures the intended taste. Give the recurring character, question, or situation a payoff that also completes the explanation.

For an institutional report or serious briefing, use direct prose, useful detail, and restrained formatting. Preserve the same attention to human consequences and mechanisms. Follow the user's requested tone when it differs from these defaults.

A comedy example is not permission to reuse private jokes, target people, or force humor into another setting. Reuse the explanatory move with an example suited to the new audience.

## Keep the evidence behind the performance

Support factual claims with the relevant source, method, comparison, and period. A sparse slide can have substantial evidence in notes or an appendix. A report can carry sustained analysis, tables, limitations, and references.

Treat older examples as evidence about communication choices. Recheck their substantive claims before using those claims in new work. Do not copy source files, personal details, or workplace information into a public output merely because they were available during review.

## Review the actual result

Read the opening and ending together. Confirm that the ending addresses the opening's question. Trace the main conclusion to its evidence and name any consequential gap.

For a deck or video, inspect the sequence with its narration or script. Check that reveals clarify the reasoning and that the final reading version makes sense on its own. Inspect the rendered artifact for legibility and layout defects.

For a report, read the argument without the design. Check that the finding, its basis, and its limits remain clear.

Deliver the requested artifact and editable source when relevant. Call out any unverified facts or unreviewed media. Keep this profile provisional until the user has judged actual outputs made with it.
